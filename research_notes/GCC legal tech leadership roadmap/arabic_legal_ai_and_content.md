# Arabic Legal AI and Arabic Legal Content (GCC first, then wider Arab world), as of October 2026

Research notes for the report writer. Every claim has a link. Where I could not verify something it is under Gaps. Older sources (before 2024) are flagged.

## Q1. How well do frontier and Arabic-focused LLMs handle Arabic legal text, and what do the Arabic legal benchmarks show?

### Takeaway
No public benchmark shows any model to be reliable at Arabic legal work without retrieval. Frontier reasoning models (GPT-4o/4.1/o3, Gemini 2.5) lead Arabic-centric open models (ALLaM, Fanar, Jais, AceGPT) on legal reasoning, often by a wide margin. Arabic-centric models now lead on general Arabic fluency boards. They catch up on legal tasks only when they are fine-tuned on the task or combined with retrieval (RAG). The most important result for a vendor is a 2026 citation study: when models answer without sources (closed-book), most of their Saudi article citations are fabricated, in both Arabic and English.

### Cited Findings
**ArabLegalEval (THIQAH + KAUST, ArabicNLP 2024)**
- It is the first Arabic legal LLM benchmark. Its tasks come from Saudi legal documents (the Ministry of Justice and the Bureau of Experts), and it has three parts: synthetic multiple-choice questions (MCQs), NajizQA (QA pairs taken from government FAQs) and Arabic translations of LegalBench tasks. Dataset and code are at github.com/Thiqah/ArabLegalEval. — [ACL Anthology PDF](https://aclanthology.org/2024.arabicnlp-1.20.pdf); [arXiv 2408.07983](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.07983v1)
- The MCQ set has 10,583 questions: 5,544 were generated by GPT-4 and 5,039 by Claude-3-opus. GPT-4o was tested only on the Claude-generated subset, to avoid self-bias. — [ACL Anthology PDF](https://aclanthology.org/2024.arabicnlp-1.20.pdf)
- MCQ accuracy:
  - GPT-4o: 76.8% zero-shot and 79.1% few-shot (the best result).
  - Command R / R+: 68.1% original, up to 72.3–73.1% with optimised prompts.
  - Llama3-8B: 68.2%, rising to 73.6% with chain-of-thought (CoT) few-shot prompts.
  - Llama3-70B: 68.17%, up to 72.34%.
  
  — [ACL Anthology PDF, Table 1](https://aclanthology.org/2024.arabicnlp-1.20.pdf)
- Arabic LegalBench F1 scores, best per task:
  - Consumer-contract QA: 90% (GPT-4 one-shot; also Llama3-70B zero-shot).
  - Contract QA: 99% (Command R+ few-shot).
  - Privacy-policy entailment: 66% at best. This was the hardest task.
  - Privacy-policy QA: 74% (GPT-4 one-shot).
  
  — [ACL Anthology PDF, Table 6](https://aclanthology.org/2024.arabicnlp-1.20.pdf)
- The authors saw smaller models reason worse when the reasoning language is Arabic (Command R+ example). The benchmark explicitly does not test retrieval of the relevant laws. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.07983v1)
- Note: ArabLegalEval tested 2024-era models. I found no published ArabLegalEval scores for Claude 4.x, GPT-5 or Gemini 2.5/3.

**ALARB (KAUST + THIQAH authors, ArabicNLP 2025)**
- It contains more than 13K Saudi commercial-court cases, each with facts, court reasoning, verdict and the regulatory clauses cited. Its tasks are verdict prediction, completing reasoning chains, and identifying the relevant article. — [arXiv 2510.00694](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.00694)
- Article identification (1,159 MCQs per task):
  - With distractors from the same regulation, every model scored above 80%; GPT-4o was best at 90.42%, then GPT-4.1 at 86.71%.
  - With semantically similar distractors drawn from across the whole corpus, the best score fell to 77.30% (GPT-4.1).
  
  — [ACL Anthology ALARB PDF](https://aclanthology.org/2025.arabicnlp-main.32.pdf)
- The authors first tried pure embedding retrieval (OpenAI text-embedding-3-large, matching case facts to articles). They reported the results were "extremely poor" and switched to MCQs. — [ACL Anthology ALARB PDF](https://aclanthology.org/2025.arabicnlp-main.32.pdf)
- After instruction-tuning on ALARB, a 12B model reached a level "comparable to that of GPT-4o" on verdict prediction and Arabic verdict generation. — [arXiv 2510.00694](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.00694)

**Citation-fabrication benchmark on the Saudi PDPL vs the GDPR (arXiv 2607.11127, 2026, single author: Noura Suliman Alrajeh)**
- Design: 120 questions, each asked in Arabic and English. Models tested: Gemini 2.5 Flash, GPT-OSS-120B and Nemotron-3-Super-120B.
- GDPR direct citation retrieval was 94–100% correct. On the Saudi PDPL, 60–77% of answers were fabricated, "invariant to query language".
- The highest fabrication rate (67%) came from mixing up the statute with its implementing regulations.
- 91% of fabricated citations were stated with confidence ≥ 0.8.
- The benchmark includes a trap: PDPL Article 32, which amendment M/148 repealed.

— [arXiv abs 2607.11127](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.11127)

- Caveats: these are free or open models, not the premium Claude, GPT-5 or Gemini Pro tiers. It is a single-author preprint.

**Islamic-law and inheritance benchmarks (relevant to personal-status and succession work)**
- Inheritance MCQ benchmark (1,000 questions, ArabicNLP 2025): o3 and Gemini 2.5 scored above 90%; ALLaM, Fanar, LLaMA and Mistral scored below 50%. — [ACL Anthology 2025.arabicnlp-main.20](https://aclanthology.org/2025.arabicnlp-main.20.pdf)
- QIAS 2025: a majority vote of Gemini Flash 2.5, Gemini Pro 2.5 and o3 reached up to 92.7% accuracy. — [arXiv 2508.15796](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.15796)
- QU-NLP at QIAS 2025: Fanar-1-9B fine-tuned with LoRA plus RAG reached 85.8% overall and 97.6% on advanced questions. On the advanced questions it beat zero-shot Gemini 2.5 and o3. — [arXiv 2508.15854](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.15854)
- IslamicLegalBench (Qatar University, 2026; 718 items, 7 schools of fiqh):
  - The best of 9 models was only 67.65% correct, with a 21.25% hallucination rate.
  - On tasks that need exact verbatim knowledge (e.g. synthesising statutory articles), hallucination reached up to 73%.
  - 6 of 9 models accepted false premises more than 40% of the time (worst: 86.27%).
  - Few-shot prompting made sycophancy worse by 3.49 percentage points.
  
  — [arXiv 2602.21226](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.21226)
- SAHM (ACL 2026), an Arabic financial and Shari'ah benchmark (14,380 items, 20 LLMs): "base Arabic models rank in the bottom 25% despite being designed for Arabic". — [ACL Anthology 2026.acl-long.1593](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.1593.pdf)
- Saudi legal judgment prediction (LJP) dataset from commercial-court judgments: fine-tuned small Llama models (3B/8B) achieved "comparable performance to larger models in task-specific contexts". — [arXiv 2501.09768](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.09768v2)
- MizanQA covers Moroccan law (MCQs from law exams). A 2025 survey of more than 40 Arabic benchmarks notes that legal benchmarks are country-specific. — [arXiv 2510.13430 survey](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.13430v1) (summarised via search; not fully read)

**Arabic-centric model landscape, 2025–2026 (general Arabic benchmarks, not legal)**
- Jais 2 (Inception/G42, MBZUAI, Cerebras): 8B and 70B models trained from scratch, released under a commercially permissive licence. Its report claims the best results among open models on OALL2 and AraGen. — [Jais 2 report, arXiv 2608.13580](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.13580.pdf)
- A trade blog reports that Jais 2 was released on 9 Dec 2025 under Apache 2.0, and gives these OALL2 scores: Jais-2-70B 79.36%, Llama-3.3-70B 74.23%, Jais-2-8B 72.40%, Fanar-1-9B-Instruct 68.97%, ALLaM-7B 67.29%. Treat these as secondary. — [Azrty blog, Oct 2026](https://www.azrty.com/blog/best-arabic-llm-for-business-jais-2-falcon-or-gpt-5)
- Falcon-H1-Arabic (TII) was released on 5 Jan 2026 in 3B, 7B and 34B sizes, with 128K–256K context.
  - OALL average: 71.47% (7B) and 75.36% (34B).
  - Dialect (AraDiCE): only low-to-mid 50s.
  
  — [AI in Arabia, Aug 2026](https://aiinarabia.com/arabic-ai/arabic-llm-leaderboard-falcon-allam-fanar-arabic-ai-quick-take-2026-08-11); [Azrty](https://www.azrty.com/blog/best-arabic-llm-for-business-jais-2-falcon-or-gpt-5)
- ALLaM (SDAIA, now run by HUMAIN) is reported in sizes up to 70B and to top ArabicMMLU. Fanar 2.0 (QCRI) reached 27B and is multimodal. — [AI in Arabia](https://aiinarabia.com/arabic-ai/arabic-llm-leaderboard-falcon-allam-fanar-arabic-ai-quick-take-2026-08-11) (secondary source)
- AraLing-Bench (Mar 2026, 0-shot): Jais-2-70B-Chat 77.33, ALLaM-7B-v2 74.67, Fanar-27B 68.67, Llama-3.3-70B 64.67, AceGPT-v2-70B 58.67. — [SOTA2 leaderboard](https://www.sota2.com/research/sota/arabic-language-evaluation-on-araling-bench)
- OALL v2 dropped machine-translated benchmarks. It added ALRAGE, an Arabic RAG generation task, and native and human-translated MMLU. Frontier closed APIs are not on this open leaderboard. — [HF blog, Feb 2025](https://huggingface.co/blog/leaderboard-arabic-v2)

### Inferences
- For legal research and drafting, base the system on a frontier reasoning model (Claude, GPT-5-class, Gemini Pro-class) with mandatory retrieval. Arabic-centric open models suit sovereign or on-premises deployments, high-volume dialect work, and fine-tuned narrow tasks such as classification, verdict-pattern extraction and inheritance computation. The ALARB 12B and Fanar-LoRA results show that narrow fine-tunes can match frontier models.
- General Arabic leaderboards (OALL, AraGen) do not predict legal accuracy. A vendor needs its own GCC legal evaluation set covering article identification, closed-book citation traps, repealed articles and statute-vs-regulation confusion.
- Because fabrication follows the jurisdiction, not the language, an English interface does not make GCC law safer.

### Gaps
- No public head-to-head of Claude 4.x / Opus, GPT-5 and Gemini 2.5/3 Pro on ArabLegalEval or ALARB. ALARB's full per-model verdict tables were not extracted.
- No published LegalBench-Arabic beyond ArabLegalEval's four translated tasks. No UAE, Qatar, Oman, Kuwait or Bahrain statutory QA benchmark found. No Egyptian or Jordanian legal benchmark found, apart from RAG studies (see Q5).
- Jais 2 and Falcon-H1 Arabic numbers partly come from secondary blogs. Primary TII and Inception announcements were not fetched.

## Q2. Common failure modes

### Takeaway
The failures that matter are confident fabricated article numbers, confusion between statute and implementing regulation, citing repealed or superseded text, accepting a false premise (sycophancy), mixing jurisdictions, weak dialect handling, and losing text during OCR. Courts in the region now name the cross-jurisdiction problem explicitly.

### Cited Findings
- Fabricated citations:
  - 60–77% of closed-book Saudi PDPL citations were fabricated; 91% of them at high confidence.
  - Statute-vs-implementing-regulations confusion produced 67% fabrication.
  - Models invented answers about repealed PDPL Article 32.
  - Models projected GDPR-only rights (portability, objection) onto the PDPL. — [arXiv 2607.11127](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.11127)
- Sycophancy: 6 of 9 models accepted false Islamic-law premises at more than 40%, and verbatim-knowledge tasks hit 73% hallucination. — [IslamicLegalBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.21226)
- Jurisdiction confusion: the QICDRC Practice Direction No. 1 of 2026 states that "AI tools do not reliably distinguish between jurisdictions and may refer to irrelevant foreign legal principles". — [QICDRC PD No.1 of 2026](https://www.qicdrc.gov.qa/sites/default/files/regulation-pdf/2026-01/Practice%20Direction%20on%20Artificial%20Intelligence.pdf)
- Fake case law in GCC courts: in Sheppard v Jillion LLC [2025] QIC, a lawyer cited non-existent QFC Court cases and was held in contempt. This is described as the first such case in MENA. — [QICDRC news, Jan 2026](https://www.qicdrc.gov.qa/media-center/news/qicdrc-issues-practice-direction-no-1-2026-and-practice-guidance-use-artificial); [Pinsent Masons, Nov 2025](https://www.pinsentmasons.com/out-law/analysis/ai-litigationarbitration-legal-challenges); [Lexis Middle East note](https://www.lexismiddleeast.com/pn/Qatar/Hallucinated_Cases_Misuse_AI_Legal_Proceedings_Sheppard_Jillion_LLC/en)
- Dialect: Falcon-H1-Arabic-34B scores in the mid-90s on STEM but in the low 50s on the AraDiCE dialect measure. — [AI in Arabia](https://aiinarabia.com/arabic-ai/arabic-llm-leaderboard-falcon-allam-fanar-arabic-ai-quick-take-2026-08-11)
- Jais 2's report notes that earlier Arabic models "largely overlooked Arabic dialects". — [Jais 2 report](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.13580.pdf)
- Arabic retrieval specifics: clitic-heavy queries (و/ف/ل), diacritics and right-to-left text break naive lexical search, and amended or superseded articles need corpus versioning. Source is a practitioner case study with self-reported figures. — [Ajaka Saudi Labor Law RAG case study, Aug 2026](https://www.mathieuajaka.com/case-studies/arabic-legal-rag/)
- Reasoning in Arabic degrades for smaller models (Command R+). — [ArabLegalEval](https://aclanthology.org/2024.arabicnlp-1.20.pdf)
- Hijri dating appears in Saudi legal citations, e.g. "Royal Decree M/19 of 1443H … amended by Royal Decree M/148 of 1444H". — [arXiv 2607.11127](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.11127)
- The Saudi Copyright Law itself is cited with dual dating: "Royal Decree No. M/41 of 2 Rajab 1424H (August 30, 2003)". — [WIPO Lex](https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/en/legislation/details/19412)
- OCR failure modes include complex fonts, numeral recognition errors, word elongation (kashida) and table structure. — [KITAB-Bench](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-acl.1135.pdf)

### Inferences
- A legal-grade system needs:
  - a canonical citation identifier for each article and each version, covering instrument type, number, year in Hijri and Gregorian, and article number;
  - a jurisdiction filter applied before retrieval;
  - links from each statute to its implementing regulations;
  - repeal and amendment flags;
  - a deterministic Hijri↔Gregorian converter (Umm al-Qura for Saudi Arabia). The model must not convert dates itself.
- Detecting false premises ("Does Article X govern Y?") should be built into answer verification.

### Gaps
- I found no published study that measures Hijri date conversion errors by LLMs in legal text. That risk is inferred, not measured.
- No quantitative study of GCC cross-jurisdiction confusion (e.g. citing a UAE article for a Saudi question) was found beyond the QICDRC statement.

## Q3. Where to obtain authoritative legal texts and judgments, and licences and copyright

### Takeaway
In the UAE and Saudi Arabia, statute expressly excludes laws and judgments from copyright protection. That exclusion does not reach a publisher's own compilation, arrangement or "innovative" effort (UAE). Every GCC state now has an official or semi-official portal. Saudi Arabia (MoJ laws portal with judgments and open data, BOE, Board of Grievances precedents) and Qatar (Al Meezan) are the richest for judgments. Commercial aggregators (Qistas, Eastlaws, Lexis Middle East) add consolidation, cross-references and now their own AI assistants, so they are both content suppliers and competitors. I could not find their AI or text-mining licence terms.

### Cited Findings
**Copyright status**
- UAE Federal Decree-Law No. 38 of 2021, Art. 3 (in force 2 Jan 2022): protection does not cover "official documents, … such as texts of laws, regulations, resolutions, international conventions, judgments, awards and decisions issued by administrative committees having judicial mandate". These items are protected, however, "should the compilation, arrangement or any effort therein be classified as innovation". — [uaelegislation.gov.ae PDF](https://uaelegislation.gov.ae/en/legislations/1534/download); [MoE English text](https://www.moet.gov.ae/documents/20121/376326/copyright.pdf/1b4d5d16-8e3c-6012-afa8-56cd4eb008da?t=1645596129916)
- Saudi Copyright Law (Royal Decree M/41 of 1424H, amended 2018), Art. 4(1): protection excludes "Laws and judicial judgments, decisions of administrative bodies, international agreements, and all official documents, as well as the official translations thereof, subject to the provisions concerning the circulation of such documents". — [WIPO Lex](https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/en/legislation/details/19412); [Saudi Embassy](https://saudiembassy.net/copyright-law)
- Saudi Arabia also protects databases "if they are creative as to selection or arrangement" (Art. 3(5)). — [WIPO Lex text](https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/en/text/206369)

**Saudi Arabia**
- The MoJ Laws portal (laws.moj.gov.sa) offers "Justice Systems" legislation with updates, "Judicial Decisions" from first-instance, appellate and Supreme Court levels, a legal terminology dictionary, and an "Open Data" library "in various formats, making it easy to process and reuse". — [laws.moj.gov.sa](https://laws.moj.gov.sa/en/); [judgments list](https://laws.moj.gov.sa/ar/JudicialDecisionsList/1)
- MoJ Open Data Policy: open data can be used "freely without technical, financial or legal restriction, also re-use it and publish it while considering the law license". The listed data includes "Rulings and precedents" and the Judicial Journal. — [MoJ Open Data Policy](https://www.moj.gov.sa/English/opendata/Pages/OpenDataPolicy.aspx)
- The Bureau of Experts (BOE) at the Council of Ministers publishes Saudi "systems" (laws), regulations and updates, searchable by classification. — [BOE](https://www.boe.gov.sa/en/Pages/default.aspx)
- The Board of Grievances (administrative courts) has a searchable judicial-precedents database (السوابق القضائية) with 1,317 records, organised by topic, year (Hijri), volume and page. — [BOG case law search](https://www.bog.gov.sa/CaseLaw/Pages/SearchCaseLaw.aspx)
- ArabLegalEval was built by scraping MoJ and BOE sites. ALARB used more than 13K Saudi commercial-court cases. This shows the data is reachable for research. — [ArabLegalEval](https://aclanthology.org/2024.arabicnlp-1.20.pdf); [ALARB](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.00694)

**UAE**
- uaelegislation.gov.ae launched on 5 Feb 2024. It is run by the General Secretariat of the Cabinet, holds federal legislation since 1971 in Arabic and English (more than 1,000 laws and regulations), includes amendment tracking, and is "associated with all local legislative platforms". — [WAM, Feb 2024](https://www.wam.ae/en/article/b1ifd8a-mohammed-bin-rashid-chairs-cabinet-meeting-reviews); [About](https://www.uaelegislation.gov.ae/en/about-us)
- The site shows recent major reforms, e.g. a new Civil Transactions Law Federal Decree-Law and new capital-markets decree-laws (2025–26). Any corpus must therefore be refreshed and versioned. — [uaelegislation.gov.ae](https://www.uaelegislation.gov.ae/en)
- The MoJ legal portal publishes Federal Supreme Court decisions (civil, criminal, constitutional), legislation-department fatwas, treaties and a judicial studies journal. — [u.ae guide](https://u.ae/ar/information-and-services/justice-safety-and-the-law/Law/how-are-uae-laws-issued-and-how-to-find-them/where-to-find-uae-federal-laws)
- In April 2025 the Cabinet approved a "Regulatory Intelligence" ecosystem. It will build "the largest national legislative database" linking federal and local laws with judicial rulings through AI. — [WAM, Apr 2025](https://www.wam.ae/en/article/bj6abdw-uae-cabinet-chaired-mohammed-bin-rashid-approves); [uaelegislation news, May 2025](https://uaelegislation.gov.ae/en/news/the-general-secretariat-of-the-cabinet-introduces-its-new-regulatory-intelligence-ecosystem-to-government-leaders-and-experts-2)
- The MoJ has a generative AI assistant called "Aisha". — [Oxford AI Justice Atlas, UAE](https://www.techandjustice.bsg.ox.ac.uk/hubfs/UAE-AI%20Justice%20Atlas-pdf.pdf?hsLang=en)

**Qatar**
- Al Meezan (almeezan.qa), run by the MoJ, holds all legislation in force, amended and repealed since 1961. It also holds Court of Cassation judgments, which the Supreme Judicial Council technical office classified and from which it extracted principles, plus legal fatwa principles and references. It has partial English. — [Al Meezan about](https://www.almeezan.qa/CustomPage.aspx?id=5&language=ar); [Al Meezan EN](https://www.almeezan.qa/Default.aspx?language=en); [Qatar University guide](https://www.qu.edu.qa/en-us/Colleges/law/students/Pages/legalresearchresources.aspx)
- The Court of Cassation technical office extracts and publishes legal principles. — [SJC Qatar](https://www.sjc.gov.qa/en/Pages/CourtOfCassation.aspx)

**Oman**
- The Supreme Judiciary Council publishes "Legal Principles" collections of the Supreme Court, including "A Selected Collection of Legal Principles Endorsed by The Supreme Court 2024–2025" and yearly criminal, labour/commercial and Sharia/civil collections back to 2011. — [SJC Oman](https://www.sjc.gov.om/InnerPage.aspx?ID=2095bd1d-1eca-4996-90e0-76652101f3c3)
- The Ministry of Justice and Legal Affairs (MJLA, created in 2020) publishes the Official Gazette. — [decree.om](https://decree.om/2023/legislative-process-in-oman/) (secondary)
- Qanoon.om is a private, free, consolidated archive of Omani legislation, run alongside Decree.om since about 2015. It is not the official portal. — [blog.decree.om](https://blog.decree.om/2022/qanoon-om-update-court-judgment/) (secondary)
- Lawrtal (Optima LegalTech) is a commercial Omani portal. It claims all Gazette legislation since 1972 (17,879 legislation items), Supreme Court rulings linked to articles, and Commercial Court judgments from 1984–2000. — [Lawrtal](https://www.lawrtal.com/en)

**Kuwait and Bahrain**
- The Kuwait MoJ publishes judgments and precedents, including principles of the Court of Cassation (Tamyeez). — [moj.gov.kw](https://www.moj.gov.kw/AR/Pages/MojPrevProvisions.aspx)
- A third-party site (kuwaitlegallibrary.com) offers 14,068 Cassation principles (1966–2013), taken from official MoJ volumes, as free reusable JSON. Its operator is unverified. — [Kuwait Legal Library](https://kuwaitlegallibrary.com/en/kuwait-legal-library-english)
- Bahrain: not verified in this session (see Gaps).

**Commercial databases, now AI competitors**
- Qistas (Amman, founded 2012):
  - Covers 9 jurisdictions: Jordan, Palestine, Saudi Arabia, UAE, Egypt, Qatar, Oman, Bahrain and Kuwait.
  - Launched an AI suite in 2024–2025: Aref (research), Taqadi (litigation), Taaqod (contracts) and Tashree (legislative).
  - Says its answers are "grounded in Qistas' verified legal corpus … not the open web", that it flags repealed or amended text, and that "practising lawyers read and classify every judgment and statute".
  
  — [Qistas FAQ](https://qistas.com/en/faq); [About](https://qistas.com/en/about); [LinkedIn launch, Dec 2025](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/qistasai_arabicfirst-legalai-arabiclaw-activity-7408398994273574912-ManB)
- In 2023, Qistas said it held more than 90,000 legislative texts and 4.1M court decisions, and that local legal content is "expensive to create, acquire and enrich". This is an older source. — [Wamda, Jul 2023](https://www.wamda.com/en/2023/07/technology-make-law-accessible)
- Eastlaws (Alexandria, founded 1995) has legislation and rulings databases for 18 Arab countries (older source, 2019). It launched the "Seshat" AI legal reasoning assistant on 17 Nov 2025. — [McGill library, 2019](https://blogs.library.mcgill.ca/islamicstudieslibrary/eastlaws-database-of/); [Eastlaws LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/eastlawsin_law-legalai-seshat-activity-7396260754628198402-mpDl); [eastlaws.com](https://eastlaws.com/Home/Index/en)
- Lexis Middle East publishes bilingual practice notes, e.g. on Sheppard v Jillion. — [Lexis Middle East](https://www.lexismiddleeast.com/pn/Qatar/Hallucinated_Cases_Misuse_AI_Legal_Proceedings_Sheppard_Jillion_LLC/en)

### Inferences
- Legal position in Saudi Arabia and the UAE:
  - The raw text of laws and judgments can be ingested and indexed without a copyright licence.
  - A vendor should still take text from official portals (or re-key it), not copy an aggregator's consolidated versions, headnotes, classifications or translations. In the UAE, an aggregator's "innovative" compilation is protected, and Saudi Arabia protects creative databases.
  - Website terms of use and anti-scraping clauses may still apply even where copyright does not.
- Content strategy:
  1. Build the official-source corpus yourself (BOE, MoJ KSA, uaelegislation, Al Meezan, Oman SJC/MJLA, Kuwait MoJ).
  2. Add a consolidation and versioning layer, with legal editors.
  3. Treat Qistas and Eastlaws as partners or licensors, or as direct competitors, since both now sell AI assistants on their own corpora.
- Official English translations (UAE, Qatar partial) are "official translations". Saudi Arabia expressly excludes official translations from protection; the UAE excludes official documents "whatever their … language". So the bilingual Arabic-English corpus is usable.

### Gaps
- Not confirmed:
  - The copyright exclusion clauses in the Oman (Royal Decree 65/2008), Qatar (Law 7/2002), Kuwait (Law 75/2019), Bahrain (Law 22/2006), Egypt (Law 82/2002) and Jordan statutes. They are likely similar, but no source was verified.
  - The terms of use or licences of the Saudi National Open Data Portal, uaelegislation.gov.ae, Al Meezan and BOE regarding bulk download or AI use.
  - The URL and contents of Bahrain's official legislation portal (Legislation and Legal Opinion Commission).
  - Whether the Dubai Court of Cassation and Abu Dhabi Judicial Department publish judgments in bulk.
  - DIFC, ADGM and QFC judgments. These are English-language common-law courts; the QICDRC judgments are public.
- Commercial licensing for AI training or RAG from Qistas, Eastlaws, Mohamoon, LexisNexis ME or Thomson Reuters Westlaw Gulf was not found publicly. The vendor must ask each directly.

## Q4. Arabic OCR quality for legal documents

### Takeaway
Modern vision-language models (Gemini, GPT-4o, fine-tuned Arabic VLMs) are far better than Tesseract-class OCR on Arabic. Even so, end-to-end PDF-to-structured-text conversion is only about 65% accurate on the main benchmark. Azure is relatively strong on diacritics. No system wins on every type of difficulty. Scanned judgments with stamps, signatures, tables and Hijri numerals need a layered pipeline with human QA on citations.

### Cited Findings
- KITAB-Bench (Findings of ACL 2025; 8,809 samples, 9 domains):
  - VLMs beat traditional OCR by about 60% on character error rate (CER).
  - The best PDF-to-Markdown result was about 65% (Gemini-2.0-Flash MARS 65.65%; GPT-4o 65.12%). Qwen2-VL-7B scored 21.42%.
  - Gemini-2.0-Flash had the lowest OCR error (CER 0.13, WER 0.32). The open AIN-7B reached WER 0.28. Tesseract had CER 0.54, EasyOCR 0.58, PaddleOCR 0.79 and Surya 4.95.
  
  — [KITAB-Bench ACL PDF](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-acl.1135.pdf); [arXiv 2502.14949](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.14949v2)
- KITAB-Bench hard cases (chrF scores; reported chrF values are low overall):
  - Azure OCR "excels remarkably in diacritics recognition (40.5) and text elongations (35.2)".
  - GPT-4o is best on font variation (30.2) and tilted text (13.1).
  - Tesseract is weak on diacritics (15.3).
  - "No single model excels across all Arabic text complexities."
  
  — [KITAB-Bench](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-acl.1135.pdf)
- Table extraction: GPT-4o 85.76% TEDS; the open AIN model 75.94% TEDS. — [KITAB-Bench](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-acl.1135.pdf)
- Baseer (Misraj, Sep 2025), a fine-tuned Arabic document-to-Markdown VLM:
  - It reports SOTA WER 0.25 on its 400-sample Misraj-DocOCR benchmark.
  - Gemini-2.5-pro is close behind, and Azure AI Document Intelligence had the lowest CER.
  - The authors found errors in KITAB-Bench ground truth (e.g. missing page numbers and small footer text) and published a corrected set.
  
  — [Baseer arXiv 2509.18174](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.18174)
- A vendor-run KITAB-Bench evaluation (not independently replicated) quotes CER figures: Gemini 2.0 Flash 13%, AIN-7B 20%, GPT-4o 31%, Azure Document Intelligence OCR 52%, Tesseract 54%. Handwriting degrades Azure by +24.5 points. — [YaiGlobal](https://yaiglobal.ai/arabic-ocr/) (vendor claim; use with caution)
- An English-focused independent benchmark (Aug 2025) found that VLM parsing (GPT-5 Mini, Gemini 2.5 Flash Lite) beat Azure Document Intelligence and Mistral-OCR on accuracy and cost. — [Dot Square Lab](https://dotsquarelab.com/resources/ai-document-intelligence-benchmark)

### Inferences
- Recommended pipeline:
  - Use the born-digital text layer when one exists (most portal PDFs and HTML).
  - Otherwise, run a primary VLM OCR (Gemini-class) with Azure Document Intelligence as a second engine for diacritics and layout, then reconcile the two outputs.
  - Detect and mask stamp and signature regions with a layout model.
  - Normalise Arabic-Indic digits.
  - Verify article numbers, dates and case numbers against metadata. Low-confidence pages go to human review.
- Arabic-specialised open VLMs (AIN, Baseer, Qari) are viable for on-premises or sovereign processing of client documents.

### Gaps
- No benchmark specifically on scanned GCC court judgments, stamps, seals or handwritten marginalia was found.
- Google Document AI and ABBYY were not in KITAB-Bench's reported OCR table, so there are no Arabic numbers for them.

## Q5. Retrieval approaches and how leading vendors ensure citation accuracy

### Takeaway
Use clause- or article-level chunking, hybrid search (BM25 plus multilingual dense embeddings, with BGE-M3 or multilingual-e5-large as the strongest open options), a multilingual reranker, and generation that must cite its sources and refuse when nothing supports an answer. Pure embedding retrieval from case facts to statutes fails on Saudi cases. Even the leading US legal RAG tools still hallucinate 17–33% of the time, so citation verification has to be a separate deterministic step.

### Cited Findings
- Systematic Arabic RAG study (2025; 6 datasets, RAGAS):
  - BGE-M3 had the best average (70.99), with multilingual-e5-large close behind (70.31).
  - Arabic-specific embedders scored lower: Arabic-Triplet-Matryoshka-V2 66.46, Arabic-mpnet 45.92.
  - Sentence-aware chunking was best.
  - The bge-reranker-v2-m3 reranker "significantly boosts faithfulness".
  
  — [arXiv 2506.06339](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.06339)
- Moroccan Family Code RAG (2.5K QA pairs, clause-based chunking):
  - Recall@1: E5-large 0.967, BGE-M3 0.953, E5-small 0.947.
  - Recall@5 was about 0.99 for all three.
  - Retrieval recall was 98% and final answer relevance 79.09%.
  
  — [TELKOMNIKA, Dec 2025](https://telkomnika.uad.ac.id/index.php/TELKOMNIKA/article/download/27400/12162)
- RAG on Egypt's 2024 Unified Insurance Law with article-level chunking: Cohere embed-multilingual-v3.0 beat AraBERT and multilingual-E5 (MRR 0.843). — [IEEE IMSA 2025](https://doi.org/10.1109/imsa65733.2025.11167628)
- Arabic lexical RAG (Riyadh dictionary): E5 sentence embeddings were best (Top-5 recall 0.88). BERT-family word embeddings (AraBERT, CAMeLBERT, AraELECTRA) did worse. — [ACL Anthology 2025.abjadnlp-1.16](https://aclanthology.org/2025.abjadnlp-1.16/)
- ALARB: embedding-only retrieval from Saudi case facts to the statute corpus with OpenAI text-embedding-3-large gave "extremely poor" results. With semantically similar distractors, article choice is about 77% accurate even for the best model. — [ALARB](https://aclanthology.org/2025.arabicnlp-main.32.pdf)
- A practitioner's reference architecture for Saudi Labor Law:
  - CAMeL Tools normalisation, clause-level chunks, BGE-M3/e5 plus BM25 fused with reciprocal rank fusion (RRF), a BGE reranker, citation-enforced generation, and refusal when the answer is outside the corpus.
  - Self-reported Hit@5 0.93 and MRR@10 0.81 on 120 bilingual queries.
  - The author warns that "retrieval metrics are not answer-correctness metrics".
  
  — [Ajaka case study](https://www.mathieuajaka.com/case-studies/arabic-legal-rag/)
- A bge-m3 fine-tune on Arabic and English legal text exists on Hugging Face (mhaseeb1604/bge-m3-law). — [HF model card](https://huggingface.co/mhaseeb1604/bge-m3-law)
- Stanford RegLab study (Journal of Empirical Legal Studies, 2025; preregistered, 200+ queries):
  - Lexis+ AI, Westlaw AI-Assisted Research and Ask Practical Law AI "each hallucinate between 17% and 33% of the time".
  - Lexis+ AI was accurate on 65% of queries, Westlaw on 42% (and hallucinated about 1/3 of the time), and Practical Law AI gave incomplete answers on 62%.
  - RAG reduces but does not eliminate hallucination.
  
  — [Magesh et al., JELS 2025](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/jels.12413)
- The PDPL study concludes that "verbatim-verification safeguards – rather than model self-confidence – must gate any institutional reliance". — [arXiv 2607.11127](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.11127)
- Regional vendors' claims:
  - Qistas: answers are "traceable to the underlying legal source", it flags repealed or amended text, and "historical versioning" gives point-in-time law.
  - Eastlaws Seshat: answers are "supported by authoritative references".
  
  — [Qistas FAQ](https://qistas.com/en/faq); [Qistas home](https://qistas.com/en); [Eastlaws](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/eastlawsin_law-legalai-seshat-activity-7396260754628198402-mpDl). These are marketing claims with no independent evaluation found.

### Inferences
What a vendor must build for trustworthy, cited Arabic research and drafting:
1. A versioned, official-source corpus. Each article is a node with jurisdiction, instrument, Hijri and Gregorian dates, in-force interval, and amending/repealing links. Each statute links to its implementing regulations and to the judgments that cite it.
2. Arabic normalisation: alef/ya/ta-marbuta, tatweel and diacritic stripping for the index only (keep the original for display), clitic segmentation, and Arabic-Indic digit handling.
3. Hybrid retrieval (BM25 plus BGE-M3 or e5-large, or a commercial multilingual embedder), a multilingual reranker, and query rewriting that identifies jurisdiction and statute.
4. Generation where every legal proposition must cite a retrieved chunk ID, plus a post-generation verifier that:
   - checks the quoted text verbatim against the source;
   - confirms the article exists and is in force on the relevant date;
   - confirms the jurisdiction matches;
   - blocks or flags uncited claims.
5. Refusal and abstention paths, with false-premise checks.
6. A GCC legal evaluation harness, kept as a continuous regression suite. It should cover citation traps, repealed articles, statute-vs-regulation confusion, cross-GCC confusion, Arabic and English parity, and dialect queries.
7. Point-in-time answers for transactions and disputes governed by an earlier version of a law. This matters given the new UAE Civil Transactions Law and frequent Saudi amendments.

### Gaps
- No Arabic-legal-specific embedding benchmark comparing commercial embedders (OpenAI, Cohere v3/v4, Voyage, Gemini embeddings) on GCC statutes was found.
- No independent evaluation of Qistas, Eastlaws Seshat or other regional legal AI tools was found.

## Q6. What lawyers and courts in the region say about trusting AI; bar and court guidance

### Takeaway
GCC guidance is concentrated in the common-law financial-centre courts:
- The DIFC Courts' 2023 note expects transparency and early disclosure.
- The QICDRC's 2026 Practice Direction followed the region's first contempt finding for fake AI citations.

Onshore Saudi Arabia, UAE and Oman courts and bars rely on general soft-law AI principles, with no lawyer-specific rules found. Every source agrees on the same duties: verify every citation against primary sources, protect confidentiality, and keep human responsibility. These map directly to product features.

### Cited Findings
- DIFC Courts Practical Guidance Note No. 2 of 2023 (21 Dec 2023; older but still current):
  - Principles: transparency, accuracy and reliability, professional obligations, and avoiding over-reliance.
  - Parties should "declare at the earliest possible opportunity" any AI use, with issues resolved by the Case Management Conference stage.
  - Practitioners should "avoid using free conversational GCGs" and use "specialist GCGs designed to produce legal content", verifying through "case law, statutes and credible legal commentary".
  - Client consent is needed before giving confidential information to a GCG.
  - The Courts may reject AI content under RDC 29.10.
  
  — [DIFC Courts PGN 2/2023](https://www.difccourts.ae/rules-decisions/practice-directions/practical-guidance-note-no-2-2023-guidelines-use-large-language-models-and-generative-ai-proceedings-difc-courts)
- QICDRC (Qatar) Practice Direction No. 1 of 2026 (issued Jan 2026):
  - AI use "is to be welcomed".
  - "All citations must be checked against primary sources or recognised legal databases".
  - Confidential or privileged information must not go into AI tools "except where private, secure AI platforms are used".
  - Disclosure is not required up front, but users must be able to identify the AI-generated portions and explain how they verified them.
  - Sanctions include costs, referral to regulators and contempt.
  
  — [QICDRC PD PDF](https://www.qicdrc.gov.qa/sites/default/files/regulation-pdf/2026-01/Practice%20Direction%20on%20Artificial%20Intelligence.pdf); [QICDRC news](https://www.qicdrc.gov.qa/media-center/news/qicdrc-issues-practice-direction-no-1-2026-and-practice-guidance-use-artificial)
- Sheppard v Jillion LLC (QFC Court, 12 Nov 2025): a lawyer was held in contempt for citing non-existent QFC cases. The court called this "the first case where this has happened in this Court" and withheld the lawyer's name. — [Pinsent Masons](https://www.pinsentmasons.com/out-law/analysis/ai-litigationarbitration-legal-challenges); [QICDRC news](https://www.qicdrc.gov.qa/media-center/news/qicdrc-issues-practice-direction-no-1-2026-and-practice-guidance-use-artificial)
- UAE: as of Sept 2025, "only the DIFC Courts have issued specific guidelines for legal practitioners on the use of AI".
  - There are also non-binding national instruments: the AI Ethics Principles (2022) and the Charter for the Development and Use of AI (2024), which stresses "the irreplaceable value of human judgment and human oversight".
  - The Dubai Judicial Institute requires a course on "Ethical AI and Algorithm Governance" (in Arabic).
  - The Abu Dhabi Judicial Academy trains lawyers on AI.
  
  — [Oxford AI Justice Atlas, UAE](https://www.techandjustice.bsg.ox.ac.uk/hubfs/UAE-AI%20Justice%20Atlas-pdf.pdf?hsLang=en)
- Saudi Arabia: "there is no clear guidance on AI misuse" in courts. SDAIA's principles (fairness, transparency, accountability, human oversight) are the expected foundation. — [Pinsent Masons, Nov 2025](https://www.pinsentmasons.com/out-law/analysis/ai-litigationarbitration-legal-challenges)
- SDAIA has issued AI Ethics Principles (Sept 2023) and Generative AI Guidelines for government and for the public (Jan 2024). — [Bird & Bird, Jan 2025](https://www.twobirds.com/en/insights/2025/united-arab-emirates/gcc-navigating-ai-regulations---the-current-landscape); [Crowell, Sept 2025](https://www.crowell.com/en/insights/client-alerts/the-middle-easts-big-bet-on-artificial-intelligence-and-data-security)
- GCC Attorneys General approved an AI ethics document. I saw only the headline and no content. — [SPA](https://www.spa.gov.sa/en/N1981309)
- Qatar Central Bank issued mandatory AI guidelines for licensed financial firms (2024). — [Crowell](https://www.crowell.com/en/insights/client-alerts/the-middle-easts-big-bet-on-artificial-intelligence-and-data-security)
- Qistas positions its product as an answer to lawyers' distrust: general AI tools "can invent citations, miss jurisdiction-specific rules, and expose confidential material". — [Qistas FAQ](https://qistas.com/en/faq)
- English High Court, Ayinde v Haringey / Al-Haroun v Qatar National Bank (June 2025): freely available generative AI "[is] not capable of conducting reliable legal research", and lawyers must check outputs against authoritative sources. This is widely cited in the Gulf because of the QNB party. — [Judgment PDF](https://websitedc.s3.amazonaws.com/documents/Ayinde_v._Haringey__Al-Haroun_v._QNB_UK_6_June_2025.pdf)

### Inferences
Product requirements that follow from the guidance:
- Every citation links to the official source text, giving one-click verification.
- An exportable "AI-use and verification log" per document, so a lawyer can meet QICDRC paragraph 26 or a DIFC disclosure.
- Private or secure deployment with no training on client data, and data residency options.
- A specialist legal corpus rather than open-web answers, which matches DIFC's advice to avoid free conversational tools.
- Clear human-in-the-loop sign-off.

### Gaps
- No surveys of GCC lawyers' attitudes or trust in AI were found in this session (e.g. Thomson Reuters or LexisNexis Middle East surveys).
- No guidance found from the Saudi Bar Association (هيئة المحامين), the UAE MoJ lawyers' affairs department, Dubai Courts, ADGM Courts, Oman, Kuwait, Bahrain, the Egyptian Bar or the Jordan Bar. ADGM Courts guidance may exist but was not checked.
- The contents of the GCC Attorneys General AI ethics document were not retrieved.
